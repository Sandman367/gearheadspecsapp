Bike Manager's Guide

Welcome aboard. You look after a bike now, which means riders trust the numbers on its page because you checked them. This guide walks through everything you can do, where to find it, and the habits that make a good manager. Read the first two sections today; come back to the rest when you need it.

What a manager does

You're the person closest to this machine on the site.

  • You keep its specs right. Riders add numbers; you check them against the manual and confirm or correct them.
  • You answer flags. When a rider thinks a value is wrong, it comes straight to you, not to a moderation queue.
  • You decide what belongs on the sheet. Fields this bike doesn't have can go; fields it's missing can be added.
  • You're a rider, not staff. Your word is about one machine. Admin handles the site itself.
The one rule: a wrong value is worse than a gap. If you're not sure, leave it empty or take it offline while you check. A blank honestly says "nobody knows yet"; a wrong number gets someone's engine hurt.

Your first day

A short checklist to get your bike in shape.

  • Open My Bikes in the menu. Your bike is listed there with how full its sheet is.
  • Open the bike's page and look at the years under its name. If the span is wrong, or it says year span unconfirmed, see When something changed by year.
  • Run the questionnaire from My Bikes, then Fill gaps if the sheet has specs your bike doesn't have, or is missing ones it does. See The questionnaire.
  • Add the main photo: Add the main photo on the bike's page.
  • Fill in the specs you know from the manual, starting with the ones in the header at the top.
  • Check My Bikes for anything waiting: flags, values riders sent in, and gaps riders asked for.

Adding and correcting values

Adding a value

On a gap, click + Add the stock value, type it the way the manual gives it, and Save stock value. Some specs have a picker instead of a text box: wire colours, fuses and fuel grade.

How sure a value is

BadgeMeans
Pending sourceSomebody entered it, nobody has checked it yet.
Mfr-sourcedYou checked it against the maker's information.
Manual-confirmedYou checked it against the service manual yourself. The strongest you can give.

Confirming or correcting what riders entered

Values riders type into gaps arrive as Pending and show up under Community Values to Review on My Bikes. Confirm the ones that match the manual, correct the ones that don't. Correcting keeps who entered the original, so nobody's history is erased.

Notes

+ Add a note puts one sentence beside a value: "measure cold", "1978 on uses the longer bolt". Notes are the cheapest way to stop the same question coming back.

Alternatives

Riders can't overwrite the stock value. What they run instead goes in as an alternative underneath it, and other riders vote. The stock line stays what the factory called for.

Tag a spec Fixed

+ Tag Fixed says there's one right answer and you set it: torque figures, part numbers, clearances. It closes the spec to alternatives and rider values. ✓ Fixed spec opens it again.

Offline, hidden and archived

None of these delete anything. Each one is a button to undo.

ButtonUse it whenRiders see
❙❙ Take offlineA value might be wrong and you're checking it, often after a flag. The field, not the value. Votes and flags pause.
Hide on an alternativeSomebody's alternative is wrong for this bike.Nothing; you still see it, to put back.
⤓ Archive valueThe spec belongs on the bike but the number in it is wrong. A gap again, so someone can fill it properly.
⤓ ArchiveThe bike simply doesn't have this part (coolant on an air-cooled engine). Nothing. It's off your sheet too.

Everything you've put away sits in the Archive (n) button at the top of the Full spec sheet, with Put back on the sheet and Put the value back. A put-back never overwrites a value someone sourced since.

▶ Put back online brings an offline spec back as it was.

Taking a whole bike off the site is admin's job. If a bike shouldn't be listed at all, message admin.

Answering flags

Flags come to you on My Bikes, under Flags on My Specs.

  • Fix Value sets the right value straight away. Use it when the manual says so.
  • ❙❙ Take offline stops the value being read as fact while you find the manual. ▶ Put online brings it back.
  • Remove alternate when the flag is about somebody's alternative, not the stock value.
  • Remove from this bike when the flag says the field doesn't belong on this bike at all.
  • Dismiss when the value is right. Say why: the rider sees your answer.
  • Contact messages the rider who flagged it, when you need more from them.
Answer within a few days, even if it's "checking, will confirm by the weekend." Fast, fair answers are what move you up the tiers.

What riders send you

  • Gaps Riders Asked For: empty specs riders want filled, most-asked first. Start at the top.
  • Specs Riders Want Added: specs riders want on your bike's sheet. Add the spec, or decline with a reason.
  • Community Values to Review: values riders typed into gaps. Confirm or correct.
  • Votes on alternatives show you what riders actually run. They never change the stock value.

Shaping the spec sheet

Adding or removing a field

Missing a spec? Under the Full spec sheet, click Ask for one this bike doesn't list. Because you look after the bike, ticking specs there adds them straight to its sheet, empty and ready to fill. A spec that isn't in that list at all goes to admin as a proposal. A spec your bike doesn't have comes off with ⤓ Archive. Nothing you do here changes any other bike.

The header

The top of the page shows the General specs plus anything you pin. + Pin to header puts a spec up there for this bike; ✓ In header on a General spec takes it out of this bike's header only.

Showing a spec under another heading

The headings button on each spec (it reads ✓ Also in … once used) shows it under more than one heading, say the spark plug under Electrical as well as Engine. It's the same value in both places.

The name riders see

Rename changes the name riders see, and + Another name adds names the bike is sold under in other markets. Search finds all of them.

When something changed by year

Two very different tools. Pick the smaller one whenever you can.

ButtonUse it when
⋮ Changed in a later year on one specOne value changed partway through (a bigger tank from 1978). The bike stays one bike; that spec gets a value per range. Merge back into one value undoes it.
Split at a year on the bikeFrom a year on it's really a different machine: new engine, new frame. The later years become their own bike with a copy of the sheet, and you manage both.
Splitting a bike is big and hard to undo. Check the years with a source first, and if the bike already has a separate record for the other years, message admin instead of splitting.

Photos, diagrams, guides and jobs

  • Photos. Add the main photo, or a photo for one model year. Whoever adds the main photo can replace it; other managers add year photos.
  • Wiring diagram. + Add the wiring diagram on any wire-colour spec. It shows on every wire it explains.
  • Guides. ⚒ Guides on a spec opens its tools and helpful links. Riders add them and vote; you can hide a dead link without losing its votes.
  • Jobs. Cards like Oil change near the top of the page gather a job's specs, tools and videos. Fill the specs and they fill the card; hide any tool or link that's wrong with its ✕.

The questionnaire

The questionnaire asks plain questions about your bike (does it have a battery? carbs or injection? chain or belt?) and puts exactly the right fields on it. Open it from My Bikes, then Fill gaps.

  • Re-running it only adds fields. Anything that no longer fits is listed for you to remove deliberately.
  • Not sure of an answer? Choose Not sure. It goes to admin rather than guessing. You'll see it under My "Not Sure" Answers.
  • A field that doesn't exist anywhere yet can be proposed to admin as a branch proposal.

Talking to other managers

The Board is for comparing notes: a wiring quirk shared across a family of bikes, a good parts source, how someone handled a tricky flag. Messages is for a private word with one person or with admin.

Your tier and badges

Your tier is worked out from your record and shown beside your name. It changes how you're shown, never what you can do.

TierWhat it takes
BronzeWhere everyone starts.
Silver100 values entered, 60% of them confirmed, 10 flags resolved, a typical answer within a week.
Gold400 values, 85% confirmed, 50 flags resolved, a typical answer within 3 days, and admin's say-so.

If more than 5% of your values get overturned after a flag, you stay Bronze until that comes down. Badges like First Hundred, Manual Hunter, Quick Draw, Clean Sheet and Wire Wizard mark milestones on your profile.

Good habits

  • Source everything. The service manual first, then the maker's parts fiche. Mark it Manual-confirmed when you checked the book.
  • Never guess a year or a number. A gap is fine. A guess isn't.
  • Take it offline, then fix it. When a flag looks right, take the value offline first so nobody uses it while you check.
  • Write it the way the next rider expects. "10W-40", "3.8 L with filter", the part number exactly as printed.
  • Leave a note when there's a catch: cold or hot, early or late bikes, with or without the filter.
  • Keep the stock line stock. What you run yourself goes in as an alternative, like everyone else's.
  • Check your dashboard regularly. Riders notice when their flags get answered.
